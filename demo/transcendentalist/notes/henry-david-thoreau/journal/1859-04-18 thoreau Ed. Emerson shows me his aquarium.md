Ed. [[Ralph Waldo Emerson|Emerson]] shows me his aquarium. He has two minnows from the brook, which I think must be the banded minnow; a little more than an inch long with very conspicuous broad black transverse bars. Some Rana sylvatica spawn just begun to flat out. Also several kinds of larvee in the water, — one very like a dragonfly, with three large feather-like appendages to the tail, small gyrinus, which he says nibbled off the legs of = the skater (?), etc., SSS etc., but no dragonfly grubs. Two salamanders, one from Ripple Lake and the other from the pool behind my house that was. One some four inches long, with a carinated and waved (crenated) edged tail as well as light-vermilion spots on the back, evidently the Salamandra dorsalis. (This I suspect is what I called S. symmetrica last fall.) (This is pale-brown above.) The other two thirds as large, a very handsome bright orange salmon, also with vermilion spots, which must be the true S. symmetrica. Both thickly sprinkled with black dots. The latter’s tail comparatively thick and straightedged.

Haynes (Heavy) says that trout spawn twice in a year, — once in October and again in the spring.

Saw snow ice a yard across to-day under the north side of a wood.
