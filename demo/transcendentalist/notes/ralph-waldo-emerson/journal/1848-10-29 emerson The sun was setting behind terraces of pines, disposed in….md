The sun was setting behind terraces of pines, disposed in groups unimaginable by Downings, or Loudons, or Capability Browns; but we kept our way and fell into the Duganne trail, as we had already seen the glimpse of his cabin in the edge of the barbarous district we had traversed. Through a clump of apple trees, over a long ridge — (query, what does Dr. Jackson call such ridges?) —OSARS ; with fair outsight of the river, and across the Nut Meadow Brook, we came out upon the banks of the river just below James Brown’s.

Ellery proposed that we should send the Horticultural Society our notes, “Took an apple near the White Pond fork of the Duganne trail, — an apple of the Beware-of-this variety, a true Louch-me-if-you-dare, or Seek-no-furtherof-this.”’
