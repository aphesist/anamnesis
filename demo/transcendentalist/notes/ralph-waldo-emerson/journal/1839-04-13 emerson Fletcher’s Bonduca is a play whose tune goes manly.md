Fletcher’s Bonduca is a play whose tune goes manly. Let the professors and reviewers who prate of strong Saxon speech read this, and write so. It is short of Shakspear’s dire style (as in Hamlet’s dialogue with his mother), but only of that. Caratach is right great, especially in the first scene, and the hard knocks which Junius and Petilius give each other recruit the ear and heart. These men are not mush.

Then I read The Coxcombs,a play which is a just encomium of woman. The situations and sentiments of Viola are genuinely pathetic and true. And the true nature of womanin her, when she asks Valerio, —

Pray what is love? for I am full of that I do not know, —

contrasts with that violated nature which Valerio considers when he says, —

Thy thoughts would be,

Like a thrice married widow, full of ends.

In Bonduca, Caratach [showing the impossibility of peace] paints the Romans out of Tacitus: —

And with those swords that know no end of battle ; Those men beside themselves allow no neighbor ; Those minds, that, where the day is, claim inheritance; And where the sun makes ripe the fruits, their harvest ; And where they march but measure out more ground To add to Rome, and here in the bowels on us, — It must not be.
