The head of Washington hangs in my diningroom for a few days past, and I cannot keep my eyes off of it. It has a certain Appalachian strength, as if it were truly the first - fruits of America, and expressed the Country. The heavy, leaden eyes turn on you, as the eyes of an ox in a pasture. And the mouth has a grayity and depth of quiet, as if this man had absorbed all the serenity of America, and left none for his restless, rickety, hysterical countrymen. Noble, aristocratic head, with all kinds of elevation in it, that come out by turns. Such majestical ironies, as he hears the day’s politics, at table. We imagine him hearing the letter of General Cass, the letter of General Scott, the letter of Mr. Pierce, the effronteries of Mr. Webster recited. This man listens like a god to these low conspirators.

[[1852-07-06 thoreau 2.30 p. m|Henry Thoreau]] rightly said, the other evening, talking of lightning-rods, that the only rod of safety was in the vertebree of his own spine.’
