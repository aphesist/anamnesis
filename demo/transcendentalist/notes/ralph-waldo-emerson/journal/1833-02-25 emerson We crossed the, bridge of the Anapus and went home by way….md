We crossed the, bridge of the Anapus and went home by way of the catacombs. We sat down on the benches of the Theatre, which was entire in the days of Nero. We askeda goatherd who smoked his pipe on the same bench what they were for? “ Per i/ mulino, mulino ;”’ we could not easily get him by our questions beyond the mill ; at last he said, “ antichit2.” On the lowest circuit of benches we read the inscriptions BASIAISEAS PIAIZTIAOS and BASIAIZZA> NEPHIAOS. There are medals with the first inscription, supposed to denote the daughter of Philistas, wife of the elder Dionysius.

In the afternoon, I went to the Museum and saw the Venus Kallipyge, dug up here in 1810, a headless beauty.
