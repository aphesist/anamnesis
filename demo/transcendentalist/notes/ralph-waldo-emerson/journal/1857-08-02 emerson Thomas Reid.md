In January, Mr. Emerson wrote to his Aunt Mary ;— “I abide in my old barrel, or, if you will, coop or tub, and mean to keep my eyes open, whether anything offers to be observed or not.” |

The question is, — Have you got the interesting facts? That yours have cost you time and labor, and that you are a person of wonderful parts, and of wonderful fame, in the society or town in which you live, is nothing to the purpose. Society is a respecter of persons, but Nature is not. ’T is fatal that I do not care a rush for all you have recorded, cannot read it, if I should try. Henry Thoreau says, ‘The Indians know better natural history than you, they with their type fish, and fingers the sons of hands.”

I should go to the naturalist with a new feeling if he had promised to teach me what birds say to each other at midsummer, and what when they convene at autumn.
