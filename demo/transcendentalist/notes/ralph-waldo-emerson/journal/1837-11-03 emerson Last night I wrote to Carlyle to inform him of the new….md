Last night I wrote to Carlyle to inform him of the new edition of his history ;* and to Mr. Landor I sent Nature and the Oration (Phi Beta Kappa) by Charles Sumner, Esq.

Our Calendar: New Year’s day; 4th March;

April Fool’s; May Day; Election;* Fourth of

July; Independence; Commencement; Phi Beta Kappa; Cattle Show; Muster; Thanksgiving; Christmas.

It is the right economy of time to do nothing by halves, nothing for show, nothing perfunctorily. If you write a letter, put your earnest meaning in, and God shall reward you by enlarging your sight. But save your thought, and you shall find it worthless, and your wordy letter worthless also. In writing a review, put in only that you have to say, only the things, and leave the consideration of the Greeks and Romans and the universal history quite out. Stop when you have done. And stop when you have begun, if it is not something to you.

Poetry precedes prose as the child sings all his words before he speaks them.
