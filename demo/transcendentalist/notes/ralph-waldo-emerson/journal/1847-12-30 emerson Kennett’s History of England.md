To Margaret Fuller [in Italy| December, 1847.

Yet I hear nothing lately of our friends F. and W. and E. and A. The goods of that country (America) are original and incommunicable to this (England); I see that well. It would give me no pleasure to bring valued persons thence, and show them to valued persons here, but lively pleasure to show to these last, those friends at home, in their own place. Shall we not yet — you also —as we used to talk — build up a reasonable society in that naked, unatmospheric land, and effectually serve one another?

I observe that many young men here look wishfully to America: I never dare say to them, Go, though I might go in their position. I observe that the idea of owning woodlands, etc., is very attractive to the English imagination.

Yet our young men find it all but impossible to live in the great continent.
