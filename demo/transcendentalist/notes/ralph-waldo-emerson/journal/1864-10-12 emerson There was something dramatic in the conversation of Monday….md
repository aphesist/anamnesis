There was something dramatic in the conversation of Monday night between Professor Goldwin Smith, Forbes, and Ward, chiefly, — the Englishman being evidently alarmed at the near prospect of the retaliation of America’s standing in the identical position soon in which Exng- Jand now and lately has stood to us, and playing the same part towards her. Forbes, a year ago, was in Liverpool and London entreating them to respect their own neutrality, and disallow the piracy, and the blockade-running, and hard measure to us in their colonial ports, etc. And now, so soon, the parts were entirely reversed, and Professor Smith was showing us the power and irritability of England and the certainty that war would follow, if we should build and arm a ship in one of our ports, send her out to sea, and aft sea sell her to their enemy, which would be a proceeding strictly in accordance with her present proclaimed law of nations. Forbes thinks the Americans are 1n such a temper toward England that they will do this if the opportunity occurs. When the American Government urged England to make a new treaty to adjust and correct this anomalous rule, the English Government refused. And ’t is only ignorance that has preverited the Rebel Confederacy from availing themselves of it.

Mr. Smith had never heard of J. J. Garth Wilkinson; nor had the Trollopes heard of Elizabeth Sheppard,* nor scarcely any English, fifteen years ago, of Browning.

At Naushon, I recall what John Smith said of the Bermudas, and | think as well of Mr. Forbes’s fences, which are cheap and steep. “No place known had better walls or a broader ditch.”

What complete men are Forbes, Agassiz, and Rockwood Hoar !

I came away from Naushen saying to myself of John Forbes, how little this man suspects, with his sympathy for men, and his respect for lettered and scientific people, that he is not likely ever to meet a man superior to himself |
