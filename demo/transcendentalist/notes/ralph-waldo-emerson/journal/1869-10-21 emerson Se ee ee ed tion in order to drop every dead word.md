Se ee ee ed tion in order to drop every dead word. Why do you not save out of your speech or thinking only the vital things, —the spirited mot which amused or warmed you when you spoke it, — because of its luck and newness? I have just been reading, in this careful book of a most intellizgent and learned man, any number of flat conventional words and sentences. ‘If a man would learn to read his own manuscript severely, — becoming really a third person, and search only for what interested him, he would blot to purpose, — and how every page would gain! Then all the words will be sprightly, and every sentence a surprise.

I will tell you what it is to be immortal, — this, namely, that I cannot read Plutarch without perpetual reminders of men and women whom I know.

Dr. Hedge tells us that the Indian asked John Eliot, “ Why God did not kill the devil?” One would like to know what was Eliot’s answer.
