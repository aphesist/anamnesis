Conjugis audisset fatum cum Porcia Bruti, Et subtracta sibi quereret arma dolor: Nondum scitis, ait, mortem non posse negari? Credideram satis hoc vos docuisse patrem. Dixit, et ardentes avido bibit ore favillas. I nunc, et ferrum, turba molesta, nega.

“ Tle ego sum nulli nugarum laude secundus,”

says this ancient Herrick in the Ode 4d Avitum, and all the English lyrists are much indebted to him; Herrick chiefly. Here is the original Doctor Fell. Ad Sabidium.

Non amo te, Sabidi, nec possum dicere quare ; Hoc tantum possum dicere, non amo te.

Martial, like AXsop, or Horace, or Homer, ora Bible, shows that one book can avail to touch all the points in the circle of daily manners, and furnish a popular literature, —as well as a hundred.

At mihi succurrit pro Ganymede manus.

“The wisdom and the arts of Athens form in all polished communities a principal object of study, and, to comprehend and to enjoy them, 1s to be a gentleman.” — Sr. Joun.
